🌊 Research Report: Factors Responsible for Flood Occurrence
Sources: NDMA, IMD, NIH, MDPI, ResearchGate, Taylor & Francis (2022–2023 papers)
🎯 Dependent Variable (Target — What We Predict)
Variable	Type	Values	Description
Flood Occurred / Flood Susceptibility	Binary	0 = No Flood, 1 = Flood	Whether a flood event occurred at a given location and time
NOTE

Some advanced studies use a Flood Susceptibility Index (FSI) as a continuous score (0.0 to 1.0) instead of binary, which gives more nuanced risk levels: Low / Moderate / High / Very High.

📊 Independent Variables (Predictors / Features)
Organized into 4 scientifically validated categories:

🏔️ Category 1 — Topographical Factors
Derived from Digital Elevation Models (DEMs) — e.g., SRTM, ASTER, Cartosat

#	Variable	Unit	Why It Matters	Impact on Flood
1	Elevation	meters (m)	Low-lying areas accumulate water; floodplains lie at low elevation	⬇️ Lower = Higher risk
2	Slope	degrees (°)	Flat slopes hold water; steep slopes cause fast runoff to low areas	⬇️ Flatter = Higher inundation risk
3	Aspect	degrees (0–360°)	Affects solar radiation → soil moisture → evapotranspiration → runoff	Indirect effect
4	Curvature (Plan/Profile)	dimensionless	Concave areas converge water flow; convex areas diverge it	Concave = Higher risk
5	Topographic Position Index (TPI)	dimensionless	Identifies valleys (-) and ridges (+); valleys are flood-prone	Negative TPI = Higher risk
6	Topographic Ruggedness Index (TRI)	dimensionless	Rough terrain = more variation in water flow paths	Complex effect
💧 Category 2 — Hydrological Factors
Most directly linked to water movement and accumulation

#	Variable	Unit	Why It Matters	Impact on Flood
7	Rainfall / Precipitation	mm	PRIMARY CAUSE — triggers all flood events; 75–90% of India's rain in monsoon season	⬆️ Higher = Higher risk
8	Rainfall Intensity	mm/hour	High-intensity short bursts cause flash floods; overwhelms drainage	⬆️ Higher = Flash flood risk
9	River / Stream Discharge	m³/s	When rivers exceed their bankfull capacity, flooding begins	⬆️ Higher = Higher risk
10	Water Level	m above MSL	Direct physical measure of flood threshold breach	⬆️ Higher = Imminent flood
11	Topographic Wetness Index (TWI)	dimensionless	TWI = ln(A / tan β); predicts soil saturation zones	⬆️ Higher = More saturation
12	Distance to Rivers/Streams	meters (m)	Areas near rivers/streams are in natural floodplains	⬇️ Closer = Higher risk
13	Drainage Density	km/km²	Dense drainage = faster water concentration; more runoff	⬆️ Higher = Higher risk
14	Stream Power Index (SPI)	dimensionless	Measures erosive energy of flowing water	⬆️ Higher = More erosion/flood
15	Soil Moisture Index	%	Pre-saturated soil has zero infiltration → all rain becomes runoff	⬆️ Higher = Higher risk
🌱 Category 3 — Environmental / Land Surface Factors
#	Variable	Unit	Why It Matters	Impact on Flood
16	Land Use / Land Cover (LULC)	Categorical	Urban/concrete = no infiltration; Forest = high absorption	Urban > Agricultural > Forest (risk)
17	NDVI (Vegetation Index)	-1 to +1	Dense vegetation intercepts rain, holds soil, slows runoff	⬇️ Higher NDVI = Lower risk
18	Soil Type / Texture	Categorical	Clay = low infiltration; Sandy = high infiltration; Peat = intermediate	Clay/Silt > Sandy (risk)
19	Soil Permeability / Infiltration Rate	mm/hr	Determines how fast water enters the ground vs becomes runoff	⬇️ Lower permeability = Higher risk
20	Impervious Surface Area (%)	%	% of concrete/paved area; urban flooding is 8x worse than natural	⬆️ Higher = Higher risk
21	Lithology (Rock Type)	Categorical	Permeable rocks (limestone) absorb more; granite = more runoff	Impermeable rocks = Higher risk
👥 Category 4 — Anthropogenic / Socio-Economic Factors
#	Variable	Unit	Why It Matters	Impact on Flood
22	Population Density	persons/km²	Affects flood impact/vulnerability; also proxy for urbanization	⬆️ Higher = More exposure
23	Infrastructure Quality	Binary/Score	Drainage systems, embankments, flood walls reduce risk	⬆️ Poor infra = Higher risk
24	Distance to Roads	meters	Roads block natural drainage channels; culverts fail during floods	⬇️ Closer = Higher risk
25	Historical Flood Frequency	count/years	Areas flooded before are geologically and geographically prone	⬆️ Higher = Higher risk
26	Dam / Reservoir Presence	Binary	Downstream of dams = high risk during sudden releases	Presence = Risk factor
27	Deforestation Rate	%/year	Reduced forest cover = less absorption = more runoff	⬆️ Higher = Higher risk

🔬 Key Scientific Findings Summary
IMPORTANT

Rainfall + Elevation + Distance to Rivers explain ~50% of all flood events globally. These three MUST be in any flood prediction model.

TIP

TWI (Topographic Wetness Index) is a single computed feature that combines slope and upstream area — it is the single most powerful derived feature in flood susceptibility studies and can be computed from a DEM using QGIS/Python.

WARNING

Features like Population Density and Infrastructure score measure flood impact / vulnerability, not flood occurrence. Include them if your target includes damage/severity, but they have low weight for pure occurrence prediction.

Sources: NIH, MDPI, Frontiers in Earth Science, Taylor & Francis, USGS, IIT Research Papers (2022–2023)
🎯 Dependent Variable (Target — What We Predict)
Variable	Type	Values	Description
Landslide Occurred	Binary	0 = No Landslide, 1 = Landslide	Whether a landslide event occurred at a given location

📊 Independent Variables (Conditioning + Triggering Factors)
Landslide factors are divided into two types:

Conditioning Factors — determine WHERE a landslide CAN happen (stable background properties)
Triggering Factors — determine WHEN a landslide WILL happen (dynamic events)
🏔️ Category 1 — Topographic / Morphometric Factors
Derived from Digital Elevation Models (DEM) — SRTM, ASTER, Cartosat

#	Variable	Unit	Why It Matters	Effect on Landslide
1	Slope Gradient	degrees (°)	🥇 MOST CRITICAL — gravity acts on steep slopes; shear stress exceeds soil resistance	⬆️ Steeper = Higher risk (>15° = vulnerable)
2	Aspect	degrees (0–360°)	Controls solar exposure → drying → vegetation type → soil moisture	South-facing in humid regions = more saturated
3	Elevation	meters (m)	Proxy for climate zone, vegetation, lithology, and erosion	Mid-range (500–2500m) = highest risk in Himalayas
4	Plan Curvature	/m	Controls convergence of runoff on slope surface	Concave = water concentrates = higher risk
5	Profile Curvature	/m	Controls acceleration of water flow down slope	Convex at top = more exposed to erosion
6	Topographic Position Index (TPI)	dimensionless	Identifies ridges, valleys, flat areas; valley floors = debris accumulation zones	Negative TPI = valley = debris deposition
7	Relative Relief	m/km²	Height difference within local area; steep local terrain = mass movement prone	Higher relief = Higher risk
8	LS Factor (Length-Slope)	dimensionless	Combined slope length + steepness; used in erosion modelling	Higher LS = More erosion = Higher risk
💧 Category 2 — Hydrological Factors
#	Variable	Unit	Why It Matters	Effect on Landslide
9	Topographic Wetness Index (TWI)	dimensionless	ln(A/tanβ) — predicts soil saturation; high TWI = water accumulates	⬆️ Higher = More saturated = Higher risk
10	Rainfall (Daily)	mm	🥇 TOP TRIGGERING FACTOR — increases pore water pressure in soil	⬆️ > 50mm/day = danger threshold
11	Antecedent Rainfall (7-day)	mm	Pre-existing soil saturation; soil already wet = less resistance to failure	⬆️ High antecedent + new rain = critical
12	Rainfall Intensity	mm/hour	High intensity > 10mm/hr triggers rapid pore pressure buildup	⬆️ Higher intensity = Flash landslide risk
13	Stream Power Index (SPI)	dimensionless	Erosive power of streams at toe of slope (undercutting)	⬆️ Higher = More undercutting risk
14	Distance to Streams	meters	Toe erosion by streams destabilizes slopes; riverbanks are prone	⬇️ Closer = Higher risk
15	Drainage Density	km/km²	Dense drainage = more surface water = more erosion potential	⬆️ Higher = Higher risk
16	Soil Moisture (%)	%	Pre-saturated soil has zero shear strength; any rain causes failure	⬆️ Higher = Higher risk
🪨 Category 3 — Geological / Soil Factors
#	Variable	Unit	Why It Matters	Effect on Landslide
17	Lithology (Rock Type)	Categorical	Weak rocks (shale, clay, soft sediments) fail easily; hard rocks (granite, basalt) resist	Shale/Clay/Schist = Very High risk
18	Soil Type / Texture	Categorical	Clay-rich soils become plastic when wet; sandy soils drain quickly	Clay/Silt = High risk; Sandy = Low risk
19	Distance to Faults	meters	Faults fracture rocks → weakened slope material	⬇️ Closer = Higher risk
20	Fault / Lineament Density	/km²	Areas with many faults have highly fractured, unstable geology	⬆️ Higher = Higher risk
🌿 Category 4 — Land Cover / Environmental Factors
#	Variable	Unit	Why It Matters	Effect on Landslide
21	NDVI (Vegetation Index)	-1 to +1	Dense roots bind soil; high NDVI = soil anchoring → slope stability	⬇️ Low NDVI = Bare = High risk
22	Land Use / Land Cover (LULC)	Categorical	Forest = stable; Agricultural/Bare/Urban = unstable	Deforested > Agricultural > Forest (risk)
23	Deforestation Rate	%/year	Removing trees destroys root network → soil loses cohesion	⬆️ Higher deforestation = Higher risk
👷 Category 5 — Anthropogenic (Human) Factors
#	Variable	Unit	Why It Matters	Effect on Landslide
24	Distance to Roads	meters	Road cutting into hillsides removes slope support; road vibration	⬇️ Closer = Higher risk (KEY in India)
25	Distance to Settlements	meters	Urban expansion onto slopes; increased load and drainage changes	⬇️ Closer = Higher risk
26	Mining / Excavation	Binary	Removes slope base support; changes drainage patterns	Presence = High risk
⚡ Category 6 — Triggering Factors (Dynamic Events)
#	Variable	Unit	Why It Matters	Effect on Landslide
27	Earthquake Intensity	Richter/MMI	Ground shaking reduces soil cohesion instantly; cracks open for water entry	⬆️ Higher magnitude = Higher trigger risk
28	Peak Ground Acceleration (PGA)	g	Seismic inertia force on slope = triggering mechanism	⬆️ Higher = Higher risk
29	Snowmelt Rate	mm/day	Rapid melt adds large water load to already-saturated slopes	⬆️ Higher = Spring landslide risk
30	Freeze-Thaw Cycles	count/year	Expansion/contraction breaks rock, reduces cohesion	⬆️ More cycles = Higher degradation
🌊 IS FLOOD A FACTOR FOR LANDSLIDES?
IMPORTANT

YES — Flooding is both a DIRECT CAUSE and a COMPANION HAZARD for landslides. The scientific literature confirms a bidirectional relationship between floods and landslides.

How Floods CAUSE Landslides:
Mechanism	Description	Impact
Pore Water Pressure Increase	Floodwater saturates soil → pore pressure pushes particles apart → reduces friction holding soil together	#1 MOST CRITICAL mechanism
Increased Soil Weight	Saturated soil is 2× heavier than dry soil → increases gravitational driving force on slope	Direct slope failure trigger
Toe Erosion / Undercutting	Floodwater erodes the base of slopes along river banks → removes physical support → slope collapses	Very common in India (riverine + hillslope)
Rapid Drawdown	When floodwater level drops quickly, pore pressure inside slope can't equalize → creates high shear stress → collapse	Occurs in post-flood period
Stream Bank Saturation	Rivers in flood overflow and saturate adjacent hill slopes from the side → triggers lateral slides	Very common in Brahmaputra, Ganga regions
How Landslides CAUSE Floods:
Mechanism	Description
Landslide Dams	Debris blocks rivers → upstream flooding (e.g., Sikkim 2023, Kedarnath 2013)
Debris Flows	Landslide + water = debris flow that floods downstream valleys
Flash Floods	Dam burst from landslide lake → catastrophic downstream flooding
🇮🇳 India-Specific Flood-Landslide Connection:
Region	Mechanism
Uttarakhand / Himachal Pradesh	Monsoon floods saturate mountain slopes → massive landslides (Kedarnath 2013, Chamoli 2021)
Kerala (Western Ghats)	Extreme rainfall → simultaneous floods + landslides (Kerala 2018: 344 deaths from landslides)
Assam / Meghalaya	Brahmaputra flooding undercuts steep valley slopes → landslides
Sikkim	Glacial lake outburst → flash flood → landslides → more flooding (cascading disaster 2023)
Northeast India	Heavy monsoon + steep terrain + deforestation = combined flood-landslide disasters annually
